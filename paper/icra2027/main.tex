\begin{abstract}
Action-chunking robot policies predict multiple future actions from each
observation.  Their execution horizon determines how long this prediction is
followed before the policy observes and replans.  Recent adaptive-execution work
primarily chooses one prefix or boundary for the chunk as a whole; even audited
methods with component-aware signals aggregate them into one synchronized
decision.  We study whether physically meaningful action groups must accept new
predictions on the same schedule.  Our fixed group-specific executor leaves the
base policy frozen: an expired group accepts its part of a fresh full chunk,
while non-expired groups retain their earlier predicted sequences.  On a frozen
ACT policy in LIBERO Object, a complete $5\!\times\!5$ arm/gripper landscape over
50 official states yields 47/50 successes for $(h_a,h_g)=(4,16)$, versus 42/50
for budget-matched global $h=4$ at approximately 0.252 policy queries per step;
the paired difference comprises five additional successes and no reversals
($p=0.0625$, exact two-sided diagnostic).  Across the remaining nine tasks, an
off-diagonal configuration is strictly best on six and tied on three, and six
tasks contain a strict off-diagonal improvement over a global point at no higher
query rate.  These static results expose systematic group-dependent execution
structure.  They motivate, but do not establish, dynamic group-specific
adaptation.
\end{abstract}
