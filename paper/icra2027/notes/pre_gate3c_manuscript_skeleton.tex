% Fresh Feedback, Retained Intent: Asymmetric Temporal Reuse for
% Action-Chunked Robot Policies

% The intervention is source age, not a 20-step gripper hold. The gripper
% prediction may change at every controller tick while the policy is queried.

\section{Introduction}

Action-chunked policies predict sequences of future robot actions from each
observation. Repeated queries therefore produce several predictions for the
same future controller time. These predictions differ in temporal source: a
new prediction uses current feedback, while an older prediction retains the
context available when its chunk was generated.

Temporal ensembling and adaptive-execution methods exploit this redundancy, but
they usually choose one temporal source or execution decision for the complete
action. A robot action is heterogeneous. Continuous end-effector motion and a
discrete gripper command can depend on different temporal information. A shared
source age may therefore be convenient without being behaviorally optimal.

Independent component refresh is not automatically beneficial. In an earlier
matched-query experiment, separately retaining arm and gripper predictions
reduced success. That intervention mixed source age with thresholding and
retention dynamics, so it could not identify why performance changed. We
instead isolate temporal source assignment with a paired $2\times2$ experiment.
Each controller step receives a fresh policy query, while the executed arm and
gripper are selected deterministically from fresh or 20-tick-old predictions.

The preregistered Gate-3B coherence contrast did not confirm that mixed-source
actions are generally harmful. Its estimate was $+.025$, with paired-state and
task-cluster 95\% intervals spanning zero. The four cells nevertheless showed
a pronounced exploratory asymmetry. Fresh arm with old gripper achieved 62\%
success, compared with 44\% for joint fresh, 40\% for joint old, and 17\% for
old arm with fresh gripper. Post-hoc marginal effects favored the fresh arm by
24.5 points and the old gripper by 20.5 points. These directional observations
motivate, but do not anticipate, the untouched-state Gate-3C confirmation.

A frozen teacher-forced audit provides a contrasting measurement view. At the
same 20-tick delay, translation and rotation errors both favored the old arm
prediction. Gripper sign error also favored the old prediction. Thus the
offline component metric reproduced the old-gripper preference but selected
the opposite arm source from Gate-3B's closed-loop marginal pattern.
Teacher-forced component accuracy and closed-loop temporal-source utility are
therefore not equivalent objectives in these frozen evaluations.

Gate-3C confirmed the selected fresh-arm, old20-gripper assignment on the
untouched primary cohort of tasks 1--9, with 14 official states per task and
126 paired blocks. FO20 achieved 80/126 successes (63.5%), compared with
53/126 (42.1%) for newest full action, 55/126 (43.7%) for full old20, 62/126
(49.2%) for age-exponential temporal ensembling, and 59/126 (46.8%) for tuned
CogACT. The four registered FO20-minus-comparator contrasts were all stable
positive under paired-block bootstrap, task-cluster bootstrap, and
leave-one-task-out criteria. This confirmation is bounded to the frozen
ACT/LIBERO system, the arm/gripper partition, and the 20-tick source gap.

The resulting contributions are an intervention-level finding that jointly
predicted heterogeneous action components can benefit from different temporal
source ages, a confirmed fresh-arm/old20-gripper executor for this system, and
an offline-versus-closed-loop measurement comparison. The paper does not claim
that all arms require freshness, all grippers require memory, that 20 ticks is
optimal, or that the effect generalizes to other embodiments or policies.

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Action chunking and temporal prediction reuse}

ACT predicts joint action chunks and optionally averages overlapping complete
action predictions. CogACT changes the scalar weight assigned to each complete
historical prediction using action similarity. Delayed-action analysis by
Lazzati et al. shows how older observation-conditioned predictions can better
match non-Markovian expert behavior. This analysis supports a role for delayed
predictions, but it does not establish a shared source preference across action
components or equivalence between delayed prediction accuracy and closed-loop
utility.

\subsection{Adaptive execution and replanning}

AAC estimates uncertainty for translation, rotation, and gripper predictions,
then combines those signals to select one execution prefix. TAS chooses one
complete cached action. AutoHorizon and PACE similarly return a shared temporal
execution decision. These approaches adapt when or how a chunk is reused, but
do not isolate component-specific source generations under matched query
cadence.

\subsection{Stale-action and chunk-consistency correction}

RTC, REMAC, A2C2, and SEAM reconcile stale chunks with current observations or
recent executed actions. SEAM can restrict overlap guidance to a subset of
action dimensions, which is the closest technical case in our bounded review.
Its reported procedure does not independently assign arm and gripper from
different source observations in a controlled $2\times2$ execution test. We are
not aware of prior controlled evaluations that independently assign temporal
source generations to heterogeneous action components. This bounded statement
must be verified again before submission and is not a firstness claim.

\section{Temporal-Source Factorial Design}

Let $E_{u,q}$ denote the action for physical controller time $u$ predicted by a
frozen policy query at source time $q$. At time $t$, the current query supplies
the fresh prediction
\begin{equation}
F_t=E_{t,t},
\end{equation}
while the query issued $d=20$ controller ticks earlier supplies
\begin{equation}
O_t=E_{t,t-20}.
\end{equation}
The source gap is one second under the audited 20-Hz controller contract. We
write superscripts $a$ and $g$ for the six-dimensional arm and scalar gripper
components. The four deterministic actions are
\begin{align}
\mathrm{FF}_t&=[F_t^a,F_t^g], &
\mathrm{OO}_t&=[O_t^a,O_t^g],\\
\mathrm{FO}_t&=[F_t^a,O_t^g], &
\mathrm{OF}_t&=[O_t^a,F_t^g].
\end{align}
Before the old source exists, all conditions execute the same fresh action.

For paired task-state block $i$, let $S_{i,c}\in\{0,1\}$ denote success in
condition $c$. Gate-3B's preregistered primary estimand was
\begin{equation}
C_{\mathrm{coherence}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_i
\left[\frac{S_{i,FF}+S_{i,OO}}{2}
-\frac{S_{i,FO}+S_{i,OF}}{2}\right].
\end{equation}
Positive values favor same-source execution after balancing the marginal
assignment of fresh and old arm and gripper sources.

After observing the completed Gate-3B cells, we characterized two directional
main effects. They were not preregistered hypotheses:
\begin{align}
M_{\mathrm{arm}}&=\frac{1}{N}\sum_i
\left[\frac{S_{i,FF}+S_{i,FO}}{2}
-\frac{S_{i,OO}+S_{i,OF}}{2}\right],\\
M_{\mathrm{grip-old}}&=\frac{1}{N}\sum_i
\left[\frac{S_{i,OO}+S_{i,FO}}{2}
-\frac{S_{i,FF}+S_{i,OF}}{2}\right].
\end{align}
We report 20,000-draw paired-block and task-cluster bootstrap intervals,
task-level effects, leave-one-task-out estimates, and exact paired-block
diagnostics. These analyses are exploratory regardless of interval width.

\subsection{Offline separability identity}

Consider any teacher-forced loss that separates by action group,
\begin{equation}
L(a^a,a^g)=L_a(a^a)+L_g(a^g).
\end{equation}
Define $A_F=L_a(F_t^a)$, $A_O=L_a(O_t^a)$,
$G_F=L_g(F_t^g)$, and $G_O=L_g(O_t^g)$. Then
\begin{align}
L(FF)&=A_F+G_F, & L(OO)&=A_O+G_O,\\
L(FO)&=A_F+G_O, & L(OF)&=A_O+G_F.
\end{align}
Consequently,
\begin{align}
&L(FF)+L(OO)-L(FO)-L(OF)\\
&=(A_F+G_F)+(A_O+G_O)-(A_F+G_O)-(A_O+G_F)=0.
\end{align}
This identity holds for every target before averaging. It does not prove that
mixed actions are harmful, beneficial, or equivalent in closed loop. It proves
only that this class of additive offline metric contains no arm--gripper term
that could represent the symmetric factorial interaction.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Gate-3B did not confirm generic composition harm}

The same-source cells averaged 42.0\% success, while the mixed-source cells
averaged 39.5\%. The preregistered coherence estimate was $+.025$. Its
paired-state 95\% bootstrap interval was $[-.030,+.085]$, and its task-cluster
interval was $[-.005,+.055]$. Both intervals included zero. Gate-3B therefore
provided suggestive but not confirmatory evidence for its generic coherence
hypothesis.

\subsection{Gate-3B showed a post-hoc directional source asymmetry}

The `FO' cell achieved 62/100 successes, compared with 44/100 for `FF', 40/100
for `OO', and 17/100 for `OF'. The post-hoc fresh-arm effect was $+.245$, with
paired-state interval $[.155,.330]$ and task-cluster interval $[.170,.330]$.
The post-hoc old-gripper effect was $+.205$, with corresponding intervals
$[.140,.275]$ and $[.105,.305]$. `FO' was at least as successful as `FF' on all
ten tasks, improving on eight and tying on two. It exceeded `OO' on all ten
tasks. These observations define the Gate-3C hypothesis; they do not confirm
it.

\subsection{Teacher-forced error selected the opposite arm source}

At $d=20$, normalized translation loss was .59578 for the fresh arm and .50667
for the old arm. Normalized rotation loss was 1.12962 and 1.09877,
respectively. Both offline arm terms favored the old source. Gripper sign error
also favored old over fresh, .27400 versus .30760. In contrast, Gate-3B's
closed-loop margins favored a fresh arm and old gripper. This mismatch shows
that the two frozen evaluations optimize different objectives. It does not
identify a causal mechanism or discredit the demonstration action.

\subsection{Gate-3C confirmed asymmetric temporal reuse}

Gate-3C evaluated five methods on the untouched primary cohort of tasks 1--9,
with 14 common official states per task. The asymmetric FO20 executor achieved
80/126 successes (63.5%). Newest full action achieved 53/126 (42.1%), full
old20 achieved 55/126 (43.7%), age-exponential temporal ensembling achieved
62/126 (49.2%), and tuned CogACT achieved 59/126 (46.8%).

The registered paired-block contrasts were all stable positive. FO20 minus
newest was $+.2143$, with paired-state interval $[+.1429,+.2937]$ and
task-cluster interval $[+.1270,+.3095]$. FO20 minus full old20 was $+.1984$,
with intervals $[+.0952,+.3016]$ and $[+.0635,+.3175]$. FO20 minus
age-exponential was $+.1429$, with intervals $[+.0476,+.2381]$ and
$[+.0159,+.2778]$. FO20 minus CogACT was $+.1667$, with intervals
$[+.0714,+.2619]$ and $[+.0397,+.2937]$. Every primary leave-one-task-out
estimate remained positive. These are paired task-state analyses; controller
frames are not treated as independent success replicates.

The effect is heterogeneous across tasks. The FO20-minus-newest differences in
task order 1--9 were $+.3571$, $+.2143$, $+.2143$, $+.5000$, $+.2857$,
$+.0714$, $+.1429$, $+.0714$, and $+.0714$. The corresponding differences
against full old20 were $+.2857$, $+.2857$, $-.2143$, $+.1429$, $0$,
$+.1429$, $+.4286$, $+.2857$, and $+.4286$. The aggregate result is therefore
not a claim of uniform task-level improvement.

\section{Discussion}

Gate-3B changes the paper's question rather than confirming its original
mechanism. Its balanced interaction does not support a general rule against
mixed-source execution. The exploratory cells instead suggest that arm motion
and gripper commands can value source age differently. A current arm prediction
may use feedback that an old prediction lacks, while an old gripper prediction
may retain temporal context associated with the demonstrated interaction. The
latter explanation is interpretive and was not measured directly.

The offline comparison sharpens this uncertainty. Older predictions better
matched the demonstration for every measured component, yet Gate-3B favored a
fresh arm in closed loop. Teacher-forced delayed prediction quality can capture
expert temporal structure without identifying the prediction that best closes
the control loop. This observation is descriptive and system-specific.

Gate-3C confirms a training-free FO20 executor for this evaluated system.
Component-specific reliability signals should not be collapsed automatically
into one source age when the components are heterogeneous. The result does not
establish a universal arm-fresh/gripper-memory law, dynamic horizon adaptation,
20-tick optimality, non-Markovian causation, or transfer to other policies,
embodiments, or real robots. The additive-loss identity remains a measurement
boundary rather than a behavioral result.
