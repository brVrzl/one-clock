\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The primary confirmation result supports component-dependent temporal
structure in an action-chunked policy. \textsc{FO20} and \textsc{Reverse20}
used the same eligible temporal relationships but assigned them to different
action groups, producing a large paired difference with task-level sign
stability. The post-hoc simple effects and positive interaction sharpen this
measurement. The temporal association for one group depended on the
relationship assigned to the other, yielding statistical non-additivity in the
frozen factorial. These results do not identify a causal mechanism or prove
that temporal effects are non-decomposable in principle.

The methodological implication is about measurement resolution. Reporting one
temporal parameter for a whole action can miss structure that appears after the
same eligible relationships are assigned by component. The same-target
contract keeps every executed component aligned to its physical target, but it
also couples source age to chunk offset. Component-resolved contrasts therefore
clarify which assignment matters in the closed loop without isolating either
temporal quantity on its own. This use is diagnostic and does not constitute a
new executor algorithm.

The cross-cohort executor result is narrower. Arm-only h16 commitment did not
improve aggregate success over \textsc{Fresh} in Object development or frozen
confirmation, while \textsc{hard-h16} remained above C2 in both. However, the
Object C2-under-\textsc{Fresh} penalty did not reproduce on confirmation.
Moreover, \textsc{hard-h16}$-$C2 does not isolate a pure gripper contribution:
the conditions differ in whole-policy query timing and in the complete
execution relationship. Fixed-h16 execution is therefore not established as a
universally preferable executor. It had the highest aggregate success in the
ACT evaluations where it was included, but its post-hoc advantage over
\textsc{FO20} was not statistically reliable. The paired interval
$\PosthocHardFOPairedCI$~pp and task-cluster interval
$\PosthocHardFOClusterCI$~pp both included zero.

These findings complement rather than contradict Zeng et
al.~\cite{zeng2026openloop}. Their study examines policy context,
non-Markovianity, and whole-policy open-loop execution. Our study instead asks
whether a whole-policy temporal setting hides action-group-dependent structure
under same-target execution. The two questions operate at different levels:
one concerns the value of whole-policy commitment under policy context, while
the other resolves how temporal relationships are assigned within the action.

Independently generated, pre-existing repeated C2 executions were available for
a reproducibility diagnostic across 109 task-state keys. Binary success agreed
in all cases, although 28 trajectories were not identical across runs and four
also differed in episode length. Thus the primary binary endpoint was
stable in the observed repeats, while closed-loop trajectories were not fully
deterministic; our inferential intervals do not explicitly model run-to-run
execution variability.

Several limits define the frozen scope. All evaluations are LIBERO simulation,
so the paper makes no physical-robot claim. The primary evidence uses frozen
ACT policies, with one SmolVLA pilot providing heterogeneous cross-policy scope
rather than replication. We study only a six-dimensional arm and
one-dimensional gripper split and make no claim about dexterous hands. Under
the same-target contract, source age and chunk offset remain coupled; the
design does not identify a pure observation-age causal effect. Confirmation
tasks were unseen to executor development but were not held out from policy
training. Finally, the Spatial panel lacks \textsc{Reverse20} and therefore
does not provide a complete factorial test.
