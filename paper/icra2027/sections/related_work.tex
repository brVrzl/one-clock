\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\subsection{Action chunking and its analysis}

ACT introduced action-chunk prediction and temporal ensembling for precise
imitation learning~\cite{zhao2023act}. We use frozen ACT policies but disable
temporal ensembling, because our question concerns which same-target chunk
entry supplies each executed component. Lazzati et al. analyze why action
chunking improves behavioral cloning~\cite{lazzati2026actionchunking}. Their
experiments examine non-Markovian expressivity, compounding error, policies
conditioned on earlier observations, and implicit ensembling across temporal
relationships. That analysis concerns the benefits of chunking and alternative
whole-policy constructions. Our study instead holds the trained chunk policy
fixed and makes action-group assignment part of the execution-time
intervention.

Zeng et al. revisit whole-policy open-loop execution through the interaction of
policy context and demonstration non-Markovianity~\cite{zeng2026openloop}. They
show that sufficiently long policy context can remove the benefit of long
open-loop prefixes, with highly reactive closed-loop execution performing best
in their settings. Our findings do not contradict that result. Zeng et al.
vary context and whole-policy execution horizon, whereas we resolve the
same-target temporal relationship by action group. The fixed-h16 evidence in
our study motivates this different measurement question; it does not establish
a universal preference for open-loop execution.

\subsection{Whole-policy execution adaptation}

Several methods adapt how much of a predicted chunk is executed before the
whole policy is queried again. AutoHorizon estimates a per-chunk horizon from
attention structure, while PACE selects replanning boundaries from phase-related
kinematics in the predicted chunk~\cite{wang2026autohorizon,nie2026pace}. DEHP
learns a lightweight horizon predictor with reinforcement learning while
freezing the base chunk policy~\cite{zhao2026dehp}. BCP similarly keeps its base
VLA frozen but learns a sequence of continue-or-replan decisions over each
chunk~\cite{xu2026bcp}. VLA-Corrector monitors visual-feature deviation and
triggers truncation and corrective replanning~\cite{pan2026vlacorrector}.
Related selection mechanisms include A3, which accepts the longest verified
action prefix~\cite{chen2026a3}. RTC addresses asynchronous inference by
freezing actions that must execute and inpainting the remainder of the next
chunk~\cite{black2025rtc}. These methods differ in signal and training, but
their temporal decision is applied to the whole predicted action.
Their objectives center on whole-policy efficiency, responsiveness, or
recovery, rather than on action-group identity as a manipulated temporal
factor.

\subsection{Heterogeneous action generation}

Robot actions are also treated heterogeneously at the representation and
generation levels. ARP models hybrid action sequences and permits different
chunk sizes for different action types~\cite{zhang2025arp}. This design shows
that a single generation granularity need not suit every action type, but it
does not test component identity as a factor in a matched execution-time
temporal intervention. Our split is not a new action representation: the policy
still predicts one seven-dimensional chunk. We project the predicted action
only when selecting same-target sources at execution time. Prior work adapts or
analyzes temporal execution primarily at the whole-policy level, while
heterogeneous-action work has focused on representation or generation
granularity. We instead use action-group identity as an experimental factor in
the execution-time temporal relationship under matched same-target semantics.
