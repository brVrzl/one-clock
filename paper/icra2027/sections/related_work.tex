\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\subsection{Action chunking and temporal ensembling}

ACT predicts action chunks to shorten the effective decision horizon and can
aggregate overlapping same-target predictions at every control step
~\cite{zhao2023act}. Diffusion Policy combines a receding-horizon controller
with a diffusion model that generates an action sequence from recent
observations~\cite{chi2023diffusionpolicy}. Both establish chunk execution as
part of the policy interface rather than a neutral implementation detail.
Recent analyses further show how action chunks encode temporal dependencies and
how policy context changes the value of open-loop commitment
~\cite{lazzati2026actionchunking,zeng2026openloop}. Our experiments keep the
trained chunk policy fixed and manipulate only the temporal source used at
execution.

\subsection{Whole-policy execution adaptation}

Adaptive executors decide how much of a predicted chunk to commit before
replanning. BID samples candidate chunks and selects among them using backward
coherence with earlier decisions and forward contrast among future plans
~\cite{liu2025bid}. AutoHorizon uses model attention to estimate a shared
predictive limit for each chunk~\cite{wang2026autohorizon}. PACE selects a
shared horizon from low-speed valleys in the predicted motion
~\cite{nie2026pace}. DEHP learns a lightweight horizon predictor with
reinforcement learning while freezing the base chunk policy
~\cite{zhao2026dehp}. BCP learns a sequence of continue-or-replan decisions
over a chunk~\cite{xu2026bcp}. VLA-Corrector monitors visual-feature deviation
and truncates a chunk when corrective replanning is indicated
~\cite{pan2026vlacorrector}. A3 instead accepts the longest action prefix that
passes its verification procedure~\cite{chen2026a3}. Adaptive Action Chunking
uses action entropy from the current prediction to select its inference-time
chunk size~\cite{liang2026aac}. These signals and objectives differ, but each
method makes a temporal decision for the predicted action as a whole.

\subsection{Stale predictions and asynchronous execution}

Staleness also arises when inference and control run on different clocks. RTC
addresses asynchronous chunk generation by preserving actions that must execute
and inpainting a consistent continuation of the next chunk
~\cite{black2025rtc}. Same-target alignment asks a complementary measurement
question: how does source age affect selected components when every command
still targets its intended physical step? The diagnostic does not isolate
latency or computational cost, and it couples observation age with prediction
lookahead.

\subsection{Heterogeneous action generation}

Robot actions can also be structured at the representation and generation
levels. ARP permits different chunk sizes for different action types
~\cite{zhang2025arp}. Mixture of Horizons processes several prediction
horizons with a shared action transformer, fuses them through a learned gate,
and can select executable prefixes from cross-horizon consensus
~\cite{jing2026moh}. Both alter the policy or action-generation structure. Our
policy remains fixed and predicts one seven-dimensional chunk; component
identity enters only when the executor selects which same-target source
supplies translation, rotation, or gripper commands.

We do not propose an alternative adaptive-horizon executor. We ask whether a
temporal decision applied to an action chunk is behaviorally uniform across its
action components, and we measure that dependence rather than optimize a new
executor. Track A tests a bounded operational consequence at fixed arm
cadences; it is not a claim of superiority over adaptive methods.
