Action-chunked robot policies are usually summarized using one temporal
execution setting for the whole action, potentially hiding different temporal
sensitivities across action groups. With frozen policies, we study same-target,
component-resolved temporal assignments through frozen cross-suite ACT
confirmation on $\ConfirmFreshBlocks$ paired task-state blocks. In the primary
comparison, assigning the old same-target relationship to the gripper rather
than the arm improved success by $\ConfirmFOReverseDelta$ percentage points
(paired 95\% CI $\ConfirmFOReversePairedCI$~pp), with positive
leave-one-task-out aggregate effects for all ten tasks. A positive post-hoc
factorial interaction of $\PosthocInteractionDelta$~pp had paired and
task-cluster bootstrap intervals excluding zero, indicating conditional
non-additivity in this frozen factorial. Across Object development and frozen
cross-suite confirmation, arm-only use of a fixed 16-step schedule yielded no
aggregate gain over fully reactive execution, whereas both-group commitment
was reliably above the arm-only decomposition; the development-set arm-only
penalty itself did not replicate. The fixed-horizon reference had the highest
aggregate ACT success where evaluated, but its advantage over the selective
old-gripper condition was not statistically reliable in either relevant ACT
cohort; accordingly, we make no universal executor recommendation.
