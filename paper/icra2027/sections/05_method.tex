\section{Group-wise Temporal Reliability Estimation}
\label{sec:method}

\textit{This section defines the proposed observation-to-horizon framework; no
estimator or adaptive scheduler was implemented or evaluated in the current
study.}  The verified executor in Sec.~\ref{sec:execution} shows how groups can
retain predictions for different fixed durations.  The proposed extension makes
those durations functions of the current policy input and predicted chunk.

\subsection{Problem formulation}

Let $x_t$ denote the input available to the frozen chunk policy at a shared
query, including the current observation and any task context used by that
policy.  The policy predicts
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathbf A}_t=\pi_\theta(x_t)
 = (\widehat{\mathbf a}_{t\mid t},\ldots,
    \widehat{\mathbf a}_{t+K-1\mid t}),
\label{eq:method_chunk}
\end{equation}
with fixed policy parameters $\theta$.  Each action is partitioned into known
physical groups $g\in\{1,\ldots,G\}$.  The estimator receives the full chunk,
but produces one reliability curve per group.  This separates the policy that
predicts the joint action from the decision that determines how long each
group's accepted subsequence is retained.

\subsection{Temporal reliability definition}

There is no ground-truth horizon label.  Instead, define a self-supervised
validity proxy from a future action target $\mathbf a^+_{t+j}$ and a
predeclared group distance $d_g$ and tolerance $\epsilon_g$:
\begin{equation}
Y_g(k)=\mathbb{1}\!\left[
\begin{aligned}
 d_g\!\left(\widehat{\mathbf a}^{(g)}_{t+j\mid t},
              \mathbf a^{+(g)}_{t+j}\right)&\leq\epsilon_g,\\[-1mm]
 &\text{for every } j=0,\ldots,k
\end{aligned}\right].
\label{eq:validity_proxy}
\end{equation}
The target may be a demonstrated future action or, in a later calibration
study, a freshly queried policy prediction.  $Y_g(k)$ is therefore a proxy for
survival of a prediction through offset $k$, not a manually assigned horizon and
not a task-success label.

The corresponding group-wise temporal reliability is
\begin{equation}
R_g(k\mid x_t,\widehat{\mathbf A}_t,g)
 = \Pr\!\left[Y_g(k)=1\mid x_t,\widehat{\mathbf A}_t,g\right].
\label{eq:reliability}
\end{equation}
This is a discrete survival function: the event is the first validity failure,
and $R_g(k)$ is the probability that failure has not occurred by offset $k$.
The definition allows nonmonotonic empirical error profiles while requiring the
survival estimate itself to represent a probability of remaining valid.

\subsection{Survival estimator}

A future estimator would map $(x_t,\widehat{\mathbf A}_t,g)$ to a discrete
failure hazard $q_{\phi,g}(k)$ and derive
\begin{equation}
R_{\phi,g}(k\mid x_t,\widehat{\mathbf A}_t,g)
 =\prod_{j=0}^{k}\left(1-q_{\phi,g}(j\mid x_t,\widehat{\mathbf A}_t,g)\right),
\label{eq:survival_estimator}
\end{equation}
where $\phi$ denotes estimator parameters.  A self-supervised objective would
use the proxy labels in Eq.~\ref{eq:validity_proxy}, with censoring handled when
the future target ends before a failure is observed.  The current paper does not
choose an architecture, fit $\phi$, or report calibration.

Three target families remain under investigation:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Future action consistency:} compare the predicted group
    action with a demonstrated future action using $d_g$ and $\epsilon_g$.
    \item \textbf{Policy re-query disagreement:} compare the stored prediction
    with the corresponding group slice of a fresh prediction at a later
    observation; this measures staleness but incurs another policy query.
    \item \textbf{Predictive uncertainty:} use an ensemble, sampled chunks, or
    model-specific denoising dispersion and calibrate it to $Y_g(k)$.
\end{enumerate}
These alternatives are design choices, not completed methods.  Gate-1 and
Gate-2A only establish that the resulting prediction-error structure can depend
on group and phase; they do not select a target or prove that it predicts task
success.

\subsection{Adaptive execution rule}

Once a reliability estimator is available, writing its fitted curve as $R_g$,
the proposed group horizon is
\begin{equation}
h_g(t)=\max\left\{k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}:
R_g(k\mid x_t,\widehat{\mathbf A}_t,g)>\tau\right\},
\label{eq:adaptive_horizon}
\end{equation}
for a predeclared threshold $\tau$.  The horizon controls how long group $g$
retains its currently accepted predicted subsequence.  It is not an independent
policy-query interval.  If any group expires, the shared frozen policy is
queried once for a new full joint chunk; only expired groups replace their action
slices, while non-expired groups continue their retained sequences, exactly as
in Sec.~\ref{sec:execution}.  Thus the framework changes group acceptance
horizons while preserving the current executor's observation and query
semantics.

The proposed chain is therefore
\emph{observation and chunk} $\rightarrow$ \emph{group-wise reliability
survival} $\rightarrow$ \emph{group acceptance horizons}.  It remains a future
method study: no learned-estimator accuracy, online distribution-shift result,
or closed-loop adaptive-execution improvement is claimed.
