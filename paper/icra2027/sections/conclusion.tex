\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Whole-action temporal parameters can hide component-dependent structure when
action groups have different temporal sensitivities. The frozen factorial
showed that component assignment matters, while the cross-cohort decomposition
showed that arm-only h16 commitment did not reproduce coherent h16 execution.
The development C2-under-\textsc{Fresh}
penalty did not generalize to frozen confirmation. Together, these results
motivate component-resolved temporal measurement for action-chunked policies,
not a universal executor rule. Because same-target execution couples source age
and chunk offset, they also do not isolate a pure observation-age effect. The
evaluation was limited to LIBERO simulation and an arm--gripper split, so the
conclusions do not extend to dexterous action spaces or physical robots.
