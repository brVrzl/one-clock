\subsection{Frozen Cross-Suite Component Factorial}

The frozen confirmation cohort provides the primary test of whether temporal
assignment depends on action-group identity. Across its $\ConfirmFreshBlocks$
paired task-state blocks, success was $\ConfirmFreshSuccesses/\ConfirmFreshBlocks$
($\ConfirmFreshRate\%$) for \textsc{Fresh},
$\ConfirmFOSuccesses/\ConfirmFOBlocks$ ($\ConfirmFORate\%$) for \textsc{FO20},
$\ConfirmReverseSuccesses/\ConfirmReverseBlocks$ ($\ConfirmReverseRate\%$) for
\textsc{Reverse20}, and $\ConfirmFullOldSuccesses/\ConfirmFullOldBlocks$
($\ConfirmFullOldRate\%$) for \textsc{FullOld20}. The detached
\textsc{hard-h16} reference reached $\ConfirmHardSuccesses/\ConfirmHardBlocks$
($\ConfirmHardRate\%$). Figure~\ref{fig:main}b preserves the four factorial
cells and keeps this reference outside the matrix.

The primary \textsc{FO20}$-$\textsc{Reverse20} contrast was
$\ConfirmFOReverseDelta$ percentage points. Its paired discordance was
$\ConfirmFOReverseDiscordance$, giving an exact two-sided McNemar
$p=\ConfirmFOReverseP$. The paired 95\% interval was
$\ConfirmFOReversePairedCI$~pp, and the task-cluster 95\% interval was
$\ConfirmFOReverseClusterCI$~pp. Aggregate effects remained positive in
$\ConfirmFOReverseLoto$ leave-one-task-out analyses. Because the two conditions
assign the same eligible old relationship to different action groups, this
contrast confirms a component-assignment effect under the frozen protocol.

The uncertainty checks address complementary levels of variation. The paired
interval preserves the task-state block as the inference unit, while the
task-cluster interval resamples tasks. Both excluded zero for the primary
contrast. The leave-one-task-out sign check further shows that no single task
was required to make the aggregate direction positive. Agreement across these
summaries is important because the experiment spans two LIBERO suites and ten
tasks rather than repeated states from one task alone.

This finding is not an \textsc{FO20} deployment recommendation. Relative to
\textsc{Fresh}, \textsc{FO20} changed success by only
$\ConfirmFOFreshDelta$~pp with $\ConfirmFOFreshDiscordance$ discordance and
$p=\ConfirmFOFreshP$. Both the paired interval
$\ConfirmFOFreshPairedCI$~pp and task-cluster interval
$\ConfirmFOFreshClusterCI$~pp included zero. The primary result is therefore
the difference between component assignments, not evidence that \textsc{FO20}
is generally preferable to reactive execution.

The detached \textsc{hard-h16} reference had the highest aggregate rate, but
its post-hoc pairwise comparisons do not establish an executor ranking.
Relative to \textsc{Fresh}, its effect was $\PosthocHardFreshDelta$~pp with
exact $p=\PosthocHardFreshP$. The paired interval
$\PosthocHardFreshPairedCI$~pp excluded zero, whereas the task-cluster interval
$\PosthocHardFreshClusterCI$~pp included zero. Relative to \textsc{FO20}, its
effect was $\PosthocHardFODelta$~pp with exact $p=\PosthocHardFOP$; both the
paired interval $\PosthocHardFOPairedCI$~pp and task-cluster interval
$\PosthocHardFOClusterCI$~pp included zero. We therefore make no reliable
advantage claim for \textsc{hard-h16} over either condition.

All factorial simple effects and the interaction described next are post-hoc
decompositions of this frozen experiment. For the arm temporal relationship,
\textsc{Reverse20}$-$\textsc{Fresh} was
$\PosthocArmFreshGripDelta$~pp with paired and task-cluster intervals
$\PosthocArmFreshGripPairedCI$ and $\PosthocArmFreshGripClusterCI$~pp.
With the old gripper relationship, \textsc{FullOld20}$-$\textsc{FO20} was
$\PosthocArmOldGripDelta$~pp. Its paired interval
$\PosthocArmOldGripPairedCI$~pp excluded zero, but its task-cluster interval
$\PosthocArmOldGripClusterCI$~pp did not. Thus the negative association for the
arm relationship was much larger with a fresh gripper relationship, without a
cross-task exclusion-of-zero claim for the old-gripper simple effect.

These are conditional contrasts within the observed factorial. Each arm
simple effect changes the arm relationship while fixing the gripper cell, and
each gripper simple effect changes the gripper relationship while fixing the
arm cell. The comparison does not separate source age from prediction offset,
because same-target execution couples them. It also does not convert either
action group into an independently randomized causal factor.

The corresponding gripper pattern also depended on the arm relationship.
With a fresh arm relationship, \textsc{FO20}$-$\textsc{Fresh} was
$\PosthocGripFreshArmDelta$~pp, and both intervals included zero. With an old
arm relationship, \textsc{FullOld20}$-$\textsc{Reverse20} was
$\PosthocGripOldArmDelta$~pp, with paired and task-cluster intervals
$\PosthocGripOldArmPairedCI$ and $\PosthocGripOldArmClusterCI$~pp. The post-hoc
interaction was $\PosthocInteractionDelta$~pp. Its paired interval was
$\PosthocInteractionPairedCI$~pp and its task-cluster interval was
$\PosthocInteractionClusterCI$~pp, both excluding zero. This positive
statistical interaction supports conditional component dependence and a
non-additive pattern in the frozen factorial. It does not establish a causal or
mechanistic interaction. For scale, the whole-action
\textsc{FullOld20}$-$\textsc{Fresh} difference was only
$\ConfirmFullOldFreshDelta$~pp, although the component-conditional simple
effects were substantially larger in magnitude. Thus a modest whole-action
contrast can obscure stronger conditional structure that becomes visible only
after resolving the action groups.

\subsection{Fixed-h16 Executor Decomposition}

Object development first motivated the arm-only decomposition. C2$-$\textsc{Fresh}
was $\ObjectCtwoFreshDelta$~pp, with a paired interval of
$\ObjectCtwoFreshPairedCI$~pp and a task-cluster interval of
$\ObjectCtwoFreshClusterCI$~pp. Both intervals were below zero. This was a
development-cohort observation rather than a cross-suite confirmation result.
Both executors queried the whole policy at every controller step. C2 kept the
gripper at the current offset-$0$ prediction while scheduling only the arm at
h16, so this development result did not depend on reducing the policy-query
rate.

The frozen confirmation cohort did not reproduce that penalty. C2$-$\textsc{Fresh}
was $\ConfirmCtwoFreshDelta$~pp, with $\ConfirmCtwoFreshDiscordance$
discordance and exact $p=\ConfirmCtwoFreshP$. The paired interval
$\ConfirmCtwoFreshPairedCI$~pp and task-cluster interval
$\ConfirmCtwoFreshClusterCI$~pp both included zero. The development-set
C2-under-\textsc{Fresh} penalty therefore did not reproduce. The near-zero
aggregate effect does not mean that C2 reproduced \textsc{Fresh} block by
block: $\ConfirmCtwoFreshDiscordantBlocks/\ConfirmCtwoFreshBlocks$ paired
confirmation blocks were discordant, split almost
symmetrically as $\ConfirmCtwoFreshDiscordance$. This count is a binary paired
outcome summary, not a continuous distance between trajectories.

The confirmation result changes the cross-cohort interpretation of C2. The
Object result showed a development penalty, whereas confirmation showed no
aggregate gain and no interval-based evidence of a penalty. The nearly balanced
discordance indicates that this null aggregate difference arose from opposing
binary changes across blocks. It does not show that C2 and \textsc{Fresh}
generated equivalent closed-loop behavior.

Coherent fixed-h16 execution remained above the arm-only decomposition in both
cohorts. In Object development, the \textsc{hard-h16}$-$C2 comparison was a
post-hoc reporting-completeness analysis of the development data, not a new
confirmation test. Its effect was $\ObjectHardCtwoDelta$~pp with
$\ObjectHardCtwoDiscordance$ discordance and exact
$p=\ObjectHardCtwoP$. The paired interval was
$\ObjectHardCtwoPairedCI$~pp, and the approved task-cluster interval was
$\ObjectHardCtwoClusterCI$~pp. Both excluded zero. On frozen confirmation,
\textsc{hard-h16}$-$C2 was $\ConfirmHardCtwoDelta$~pp with
$\ConfirmHardCtwoDiscordance$ discordance and exact
$p=\ConfirmHardCtwoP$. Its paired and task-cluster intervals were
$\ConfirmHardCtwoPairedCI$ and $\ConfirmHardCtwoClusterCI$~pp. Aggregate
effects stayed positive in $\ConfirmHardCtwoLoto$ leave-one-task-out analyses,
with a minimum of $\ConfirmHardCtwoLotoMinimum$~pp.

On the frozen cross-suite confirmation cohort, arm commitment alone does not reproduce
the benefit of coherent fixed-h16 execution, although it does not reliably underperform
fully reactive execution as it did on the Object development cohort.
Arm-only h16 commitment produced no aggregate gain over Fresh in either cohort,
while coherent fixed-h16 execution was reliably above the arm-only decomposition
in both. This is a bounded executor decomposition. It does not identify a pure
gripper contribution because \textsc{hard-h16} and C2 differ in their complete
execution relationship and policy-query schedule, not only in one independently
randomized action group.

\subsection{Scope and Negative Results}

The LIBERO-Spatial scope panel did not favor \textsc{FO20} over reactive
execution. \textsc{Fresh} and \textsc{FO20} each succeeded on $40/100$ blocks;
their discordance was $5{:}5$ with exact $p=1$. \textsc{Reverse20} was not run,
so Spatial is not a full factorial replication.

This Spatial result is a negative scope check for the available
\textsc{FO20}$-$\textsc{Fresh} comparison. Without \textsc{Reverse20}, it
cannot test whether assigning the old relationship to the gripper differs from
assigning it to the arm. Its null pairwise result therefore bounds deployment
claims without contradicting the complete confirmation factorial.

The frozen SmolVLA pilot supplies directional cross-policy scope rather than a
replication across policy classes. At $d=16$, the pooled paired
FO$-$Reverse direction was positive but did not reach conventional
significance, and suite-level behavior was heterogeneous. These negative and
mixed scope results bound the ACT factorial claim rather than extending it to a
universal component rule. The pilot used a different policy class, so its
direction is relevant to scope but does not reproduce the ACT finding under a
matched policy or checkpoint.
