# ICRA 2027 Zero-Trust Re-audit — 2026-08-21

**RECOMMENDED DIRECTION:** Do **not** implement a Gate-3 policy yet. Run Gate-3A:
a dense, every-demonstration-step temporal-prediction cache on a task-balanced
subset, followed by control-aligned scalar/group/consistency-constrained oracle
and simple-baseline analysis. The conditional paper direction is
consistency-constrained temporal selection, not dynamic horizon alone.

**CONFIDENCE:** High that the existing paper story is not supported and that
Gate-3A is the correct next test; low-to-moderate that the proposed method will
survive that test.

**MOST IMPORTANT VERIFIED FINDING:** The only direct matched-query test of a
group-wise selective retain/refresh rule is strongly negative: −.26, −.20, and
−.29 absolute success at query periods 4, 8, and 16, with both episode- and
task-cluster bootstrap intervals below zero. Provenance: [`rollout_evidence_recomputed.json`](audit_outputs/rollout_evidence_recomputed.json).

**MOST IMPORTANT PREVIOUS CLAIM THAT FAILED AUDIT:** Gate-2B did not establish
sharp phase/group optima or a late-phase coupling anomaly. Its “phases” were
fixed fractions of the 280-step limit, the late group point maximum was `(16,8)`
rather than `(16,16)`, most candidates were tied, and combined maps did not
robustly beat static controls. Provenance: [`gate2b_recomputed.json`](audit_outputs/gate2b_recomputed.json)
and [`gate2b_candidate_support_curves.csv`](audit_outputs/gate2b_candidate_support_curves.csv).

**BIGGEST UNKNOWN:** Whether lower teacher-forced temporal-expert error causes or
predicts higher closed-loop success.

**CHEAPEST DECISIVE NEXT TEST:** Cache dense ACT chunks for a small preregistered
task-balanced cohort (for example, five complete held-out demonstration episodes
per task), then compare newest, exact ACT exponential, uniform, similarity,
fixed-age, scalar oracle, independent-group oracle, and
consistency-constrained-group oracle under physical-time-aligned,
control-aligned losses. No router training is needed.

**KILL CONDITION:** Stop the group-routing direction if dense independent-group
oracle advantage over scalar oracle is absent or unstable under both
normalized-dimension and control-aligned losses, if a consistency constraint
removes the advantage, or if a simple similarity/smoothness baseline matches it.
Even after an offline pass, stop the policy-improvement claim if a small paired
query-matched rollout does not beat the strongest scalar baseline.

- Dataset: pinned revision `cbf7122d530a6864644b13cddff9cf463f8f0bd8`,
  454 successful episodes, 66,984 frames, ten tasks, 10 Hz.

### Action contract

LIBERO action is seven dimensional, ordered `translation[0:3] |
axis-angle rotation[3:6] | gripper[6]`. Arm commands are relative Cartesian
deltas; controller clipping applies. Gripper execution consumes sign, not
continuous magnitude. Among 709,241 historical actions, per-dimension values
outside [-1,1] are `[95,874,8628,0,0,0,551635]`; 77.78% of gripper scalars exceed
unit magnitude without changing the sign-based command. Provenance:
[`rollout_evidence_recomputed.json`](audit_outputs/rollout_evidence_recomputed.json).

The reported RoboTwin order `left_arm[6] | left_gripper[1] | right_arm[6] |
right_gripper[1]` matches the current upstream `take_action` path, but that path
uses absolute qpos targets and environment interpolation. It is not historically
linked to a valid project rollout, so no RoboTwin result enters the fact sheet.

### Execution path

`observation → preprocessing → ACT full chunk → FixedChunkExecutor → group slice
composition → environment step` is correctly traced. When any group expires,
ACT is queried once; expired groups install fresh slices while unexpired groups
retain their old chunk. A resulting action can combine source generations.
Diagonal group schedules reproduce global schedules. This contract is not
wrong, so the audit continues—but mixed-generation composition is a mechanism,
not a neutral refactor.

1. **Does execution horizon affect success?** Yes for this checkpoint and task 0:

3. **Task-level survival?** Horizon landscapes differ by task. The common group

7. **Implementation artifact?** Diagonal equivalence and schedules validate. The
   executor intentionally mixes source generations; that can cause performance
   differences and must be treated as the intervention.

## 7. Intermediate Gate re-audit

### Definitions

- Prediction horizon: ACT outputs 100 future actions.
- Execution horizon: prefix length committed before replacement for a group.
- Query interval: steps between ACT forward passes.
- Temporal age: target action time minus source-observation time.
- Action age: same only when referring to a particular candidate action, not the
  chunk as a whole.

These were conflated in earlier narratives. At 10 Hz demonstrations and 20 Hz
rollouts, equal step counts also mean different seconds.

### Gate-1

Gate-1 uses 1,098 sparse source points from successful demonstrations and frozen
ACT chunks. It compares prediction to the demonstrated future action. The
observed group curves are reproducible and nonmonotonic, but they depend on
normalization/action representation and are teacher-forced. They do not show a
preferred closed-loop execution horizon.

### Gate-2A and support equation

For translation and rotation groups:

\[
e_{trans}(t,k)=\sqrt{\frac{1}{3}\sum_{d=0}^{2}
((\hat a_{t,k,d}-a^*_{t+k,d})/\sigma_d)^2},
\]

with the analogous sum over dimensions 3–5 for rotation. Arm point validity is
`e_trans <= theta AND e_rot <= theta`. Gripper point validity is normalized
absolute error below theta **and** sign agreement. Prefix support is

\[
Y_g(t,k)=\bigwedge_{j=0}^{k} V_g(t,j),
\]

with censored offsets masked. Equation/code provenance:
[`reliability_and_smoothness_recomputed.json`](audit_outputs/reliability_and_smoothness_recomputed.json).

At theta=1, 259,279 observed pairs produce:

| Group | Point-valid rate | Prefix-valid rate | Mean decoded prefix | Point positives erased after earlier failure | Samples with later recovery |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Arm | .5596 | .1309 | 9.459 | 76.6% | 91.5% |
| Gripper | .7186 | .2940 | 20.652 | 59.1% | 86.8% |

Arm mean prefix changes from 1.77 at theta=.5 to 9.46 at 1, 28.30 at 1.5,
and 51.01 at 2. The gripper magnitude condition at theta=1 changes almost
nothing beyond sign. Thus “support” is a thresholded, cumulative proxy, not an
intrinsic policy horizon.

Under thirds, arm/gripper censor-aware AUCs over k=0..37 are
`[.177,.267,.275]` and `[.369,.591,.481]`; under quartiles they are
`[.186,.182,.384,.220]` and `[.402,.393,.780,.233]`. Segmentation materially
changes the apparent phase pattern. Continuous-time deciles are available in the
audit JSON; task-specific progress/contact labels do not exist.

### Phase and sensitivity audit

Phase is `environment_step / 280`: early 0–93, middle 94–186, late 187–279. It
is not normalized by realized episode length and not semantic progress. Only
69–77 of 230 conditions reach late in representative candidates, so late results
condition on long/hard trajectories. Because no per-step traces were saved,
quartiles, continuous time, event-aligned progress, task-specific progress, and
no-phase resegmentation cannot be recomputed. A learned segmentation is also
impossible without new observations/logs.

Recomputed point maps are global `early4/middle16/late8` and group

descriptively tied with selected in each phase; tied group candidates are 11/25,
3/25, and 22/25. In 1,000 half-state splits, late global selection is h8 502
times and h16 498; late group selection chooses `(16,16)` 445 times and `(16,8)`
432. These are broad/noisy plateaus, not sharply identified optima.

difference +.014, task-bootstrap CI [-.045,.070]. Combined group map is .747
versus static `(4,16)` .734, difference +.013, CI [-.017,.045]. The same
conditions selected and evaluated the maps.

### Late anomaly decision

The premise `late global8 / arm16 / gripper16` is false: the group point maximum
uses gripper8, and `(16,8)` versus `(16,16)` differs by only .005 with a
difference interval spanning zero. Dimensional weighting, thresholding, action
scale, pooling, grid discretization, phase selection, task imbalance, noise, and
selection bias were not rejected; several are directly present. No coupling
claim is allowed. A direct composition test must compare joint-source scalar
selection, independent preferred group sources, and a consistency-constrained
composition on the same query schedule.

There is only one arm and one gripper in valid LIBERO data. Left/right pooling or
separation cannot be audited.

## 9. Reliability-estimator audit

### Target drift

The original target was demonstration consistency. Y_refresh instead compares a
stale source prediction to the first action from the same ACT policy freshly
queried at a future demonstration observation. It is neither expert correctness
nor closed-loop value of re-querying. Original fresh-action arrays and target
construction code are missing.

Against the demonstration target:

| Group | Cell agreement | Horizon Spearman | Mean demo horizon | Mean refresh horizon | Horizon MAE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Arm | .4623 | .0224 | 9.459 | 45.047 | 36.740 |
| Gripper | .8210 | .4068 | 20.652 | 22.365 | 12.232 |

Source: [`reliability_and_smoothness_recomputed.json`](audit_outputs/reliability_and_smoothness_recomputed.json).

### Attempts and protocol

The completed estimators use 7,480 source/group rows from 3,740 source windows
over 454 episodes. Splitting is by episode (6,102 train, 696 validation, 682
test rows), preventing episode leakage. Inputs are flattened normalized source
chunk plus group one-hot; later ablations add source state, frozen ACT latent, or
both. Architectures include an independent shared MLP and a monotone conditional
survival MLP, each with hidden dimensions 64/32, BCE-style probability training,
three seeds, early stopping, and validation-selected decoding threshold. Priors
are explicit baselines. There is no task-held-out split, rollout outcome target,
or class-balanced causal intervention.

Exact chunk-only replication reports pooled test AUROC .9224, AUPRC .9109,
Brier .1136, ECE .0314. Decoded prefix-horizon MAE is 16.71,
within±2 is .2009, and Spearman is .6088. The preregistered usefulness rule
required within±2 at least .50, so the scientific verdict is **PARTIAL**. Adding
source state/ACT latent does not improve the primary criteria.

Thus “reliability is unlearnable” is contradicted for Y_refresh cells, while
“the estimator worked as a horizon controller” is unsupported. The result says
that a lossy binary agreement target is predictable at cell level but poorly
decoded into a usable prefix horizon. It says nothing about temporal support in
general or rollout success.

### Information destruction

The pipeline `continuous error → thresholded point bit → cumulative prefix bit`
retains at most one bit per cell and then sets every post-first-failure cell to
zero. At theta=1 this erases 76.6% of later arm-valid and 59.1% of later
gripper-valid cells, even though later recovery occurs in 91.5% and 86.8% of
samples. This target construction—not absence of temporal information—is the
leading explanation for horizon-recovery failure.

## 11. Unsupported / contradicted conclusions

Unsupported:

- a universal group schedule improves on the best global schedule;
- Gate-2B phase conditioning improves success;
- normalized time bins are semantic task phases;
- arm/gripper marginal optima prove intrinsic group timescales;
- the late cell proves coupling/non-additivity;
- support predicts an execution horizon that improves success;
- offline MSE gain is policy gain;
- current evidence generalizes to left/right arms, VLA, RoboTwin, dexterous, or
  real-robot systems.

Contradicted:

- Gate-2B used realized normalized thirds;
- late Gate-2B group optimum was `(16,16)`;
- Gate-2B optima were sharp;
- reliability was generally unlearnable;
- continuous LIBERO gripper magnitude is control-aligned;
- the tested matched-query selective group rule improves success;
- a generic learned temporal expert gate or generic dynamic horizon is novel.

timing, chunk-boundary discontinuity, finite-sample selection, cross-generation
inconsistency, demonstration multimodality/noise, chunk-position bias, action
scaling, semantic events, 10/20 Hz mismatch, and closed-loop distribution
correction.

Each entry records compatible and incompatible observations, a falsifiable
prediction, rival explanation, and smallest discriminating experiment. The
current most economical *hypothesis*, not fact, is that temporal ensembles reduce
variance; similarity avoids mode mixing; unconstrained group mixing can be
inconsistent; and old support/phase metrics obscure these effects.

## 13. Updated literature landscape

The claim-by-claim source audit is
[`literature_claims_reaudit.md`](literature_claims_reaudit.md); the required
comparison table is [`related_work_matrix.md`](related_work_matrix.md).

Primary-source conclusions:

- [ACT](https://roboticsproceedings.org/rss19/p016.html) already exponentially
  ensembles overlapping predictions for the same physical action.
- [CogACT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19650) uses similarity-adaptive action
  ensembling over current and historical predictions.
- [TAS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04421) explicitly caches predictions from
  multiple timesteps and learns a selector; it is the closest temporal-routing
  collision.
- [MoH](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19433), accepted at ICML 2026, trains explicit
  horizon experts and a fusion gate.
- [SGAC](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/79ce24f9e8d3c4ff5919240eac78a782-Abstract-Conference.html),
  [AutoHorizon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21445),
  [AAC](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Liang_Adaptive_Action_Chunking_at_Inference-time_for_Vision-Language-Action_Models_CVPR_2026_paper.html),
  [A3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11567),
  [PACE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00537),
  [DVAC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03847),
  [DEHP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11408), and
  [BCP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03483) already cover state-dependent scalar
  execution/replanning through similarity, attention, entropy, verification,
  kinematics, denoising variance, and learned/RL heads.
- [RTC](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/300ccb2187dedd4edcc07f7e76d8e553-Abstract-Conference.html),
  [A2C2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23224),
  [REMAC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20130), and
  [SEAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04609) address stale/asynchronous chunks,
  correction, or cross-chunk consistency.
- [Lazzati et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02547) directly analyze why
  action chunking works and identify implicit ensembling across delayed temporal
  relationships as one factor.

**Answer:** dynamic execution horizon alone is not a defensible ICRA 2027
novelty. This conclusion is high confidence.

## 16. Oracle headroom analysis plan/results

### Available sparse result

The new audit uses 5,118 target actions from 41 held-out episodes, with 2–6
saved experts per target (mean 4.456). Sources are sparse, approximately
25-step-spaced plus phase samples; this is not dense ACT TE.

| Method | Group-balanced normalized error |
|---|---:|
| Newest only | .7728 |
| Oldest valid | .9947 |
| Uniform ensemble | .7404 |
| ACT-style exponential coefficient .03 | .7112 |
| Validation-selected similarity ensemble | .7084 |
| Phase-conditioned group age | .7467 |
| Hard scalar oracle | .4788 |
| Hard independent-group oracle | .4130 |
| Convex scalar oracle | .4667 |
| Convex independent-group oracle | .4046 |

Similarity minus newest is −.0644 with episode-bootstrap CI
[-.0864,−.0453]. Defined headroom:

- routing headroom = best non-oracle − group oracle = .2954;
- groupwise advantage = scalar oracle − group oracle = .0658 under
  group-balanced loss and .0301 under normalized-dimension loss;
- contextual advantage = best fixed group age − contextual group oracle = .3606.

These are unattainable teacher-forced bounds vulnerable to demonstration noise,
multimodality, sparse candidate bias, and physically inconsistent group mixing.
They do not predict success. Full per-task/phase results are in
[`sparse_temporal_expert_oracle.json`](audit_outputs/sparse_temporal_expert_oracle.json).

### Required dense plan

Run every requested baseline on dense predictions; split by episode and task;
use task/episode bootstrap; align temporal age in seconds; use translation,
rotation, and gripper sign/transition losses; sweep justified weights; condition
on smoothness/disagreement/events; and compare scalar, independent group, and
consistency-constrained oracle frontiers. Only then train a selector.

### Smoothness confound

Demo arm horizon correlations are −.284 velocity, −.249 acceleration, −.234
jerk, −.443 future-five-step velocity, and −.434 future variance. A causal
linear ridge gives test R² .037; adding future explanatory features gives .114.
Gripper future change correlation is −.387 and explanatory R² .109. Smoothness
is real but the tested linear features do not explain most variation. A
predeclared nonlinear smoothness-only baseline is still required.

### Offline error versus control

No matched artifact permits correlation of offline routing gain with rollout
success gain. Demonstration actions may be multimodal; gripper MSE is
misaligned; teacher forcing stays on the demonstration manifold. Therefore no
control claim is made.

## 17. Recommended paper direction

Conditional direction: **Consistency-Constrained Temporal Selection (CCTS)**.
Start with a full-action temporal selector. Permit a group-specific residual
weight only when predicted control-aligned gain exceeds a joint-source
incompatibility cost. Keep ACT frozen. The novelty must be the formal and
empirical group-consistency mechanism—not “temporal experts,” “adaptive
horizon,” or a generic gate.

The complete paper-before-experiments specification, including problem,
verified observation, hypothesis, mechanism, method equation, novelty, killer
experiment, kill conditions, baselines, ablations, generalization, real-robot
requirements, and Figure 1, is
[`icra2027_candidate_spec.md`](icra2027_candidate_spec.md).

## 18. Fallback

If group advantage fails, drop the “one clock” thesis. The fallback is a
mechanism-focused scalar temporal-ensemble study: when do ACT temporal experts
help, when does similarity avoid mode mixing, how do boundary discontinuity and
fresh observations trade off, and does any offline signal predict paired
closed-loop gain? This could support an analysis-plus-baseline paper, but
temporal ensembling itself is not novel.

## 19. Stop-doing list

- No new binary prefix-reliability head on the current target.

- No Gate-2B point maps as labels or “oracle” ground truth.
- No semantic interpretation of fixed time-limit thirds.
- No group-timescale claim from marginal argmax differences.
- No unconstrained independently aged group composition.
- No continuous gripper-magnitude control objective in LIBERO.
- No policy-improvement claim from offline MSE.
- No RoboTwin/VLA/real-robot generalization before exact contracts and evidence.
- No combined freshness+routing model before each mechanism passes alone.

## 20. Next experimental Gate

**Gate-3A — Dense temporal evidence, offline, no training.**

1. Freeze and hash dataset, checkpoint, environment, project commit, task list,
   and source cohort before inference.
2. Query ACT at every demonstration step in a task-balanced subset; save full
   chunks, observation/source IDs, exact physical time, valid offset mask, and
   hashes.
3. Predefine train/validation/test episode splits and, if feasible, a held-out
   task split.
4. Compute full age curves and all scalar/non-oracle/oracle baselines.
5. Use control-aligned loss and weight/segmentation/time-resampling sensitivity.
6. Annotate a small blinded contact/gripper/object-motion event cohort.
7. Measure smoothness/disagreement explanatory baselines.
8. Apply the kill conditions before writing policy code.
9. If and only if the gate passes, prototype scalar selector, independent group
